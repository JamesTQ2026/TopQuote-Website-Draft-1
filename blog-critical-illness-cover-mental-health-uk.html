Does Critical Illness Cover Pay Out for Mental Health Conditions?

T
Tanweer Hussain
Protection Expert
7 April 2026 4 min read

Critical illness cover is a type of protection insurance that pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a specified serious medical condition. This article explains in general terms how critical illness policies typically approach mental health conditions, and what other types of protection insurance may be relevant. It is intended as general information only and does not constitute financial advice.

What Is Critical Illness Cover?

A critical illness policy pays a tax-free lump sum if the policyholder is diagnosed with one of the medical conditions listed in the policy during the policy term. The list of covered conditions, and the definitions used, are set out in the policy documents and vary between providers. Conditions commonly included in standard critical illness policies include certain types of cancer, heart attack, and stroke, among others.

The number of conditions covered and the specific definitions used differ significantly between policies. Some policies include a larger number of conditions than others, and some providers offer enhanced or additional payments for certain diagnoses. The policy documents are the definitive source of information about what is and is not covered.

Are Mental Health Conditions Typically Included?

In the majority of standard critical illness policies available in the UK market, mental health conditions are not included in the list of covered conditions. Conditions such as depression, anxiety disorders, bipolar disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, and others are generally not covered by standard critical illness policies.

This is a general observation about the market. Individual policies differ, and the only way to confirm what a specific policy covers is to read the policy documents carefully or to seek independent advice from a qualified professional.

Why Mental Health Conditions Are Generally Not Included

Critical illness policies are structured around discrete, diagnosable medical events that can be defined using clear clinical criteria. Mental health conditions present differently, can vary significantly in severity, and are typically assessed using clinical judgement rather than objective diagnostic tests alone. These characteristics have historically made it difficult to define mental health conditions in a way that is suitable for use in a critical illness policy structure.

This position is evolving. Some providers have begun to develop products that incorporate mental health provisions to varying degrees. These remain less common than standard critical illness policies and the scope of any mental health cover included varies.

Income Protection and Mental Health

Income protection insurance operates differently from critical illness cover. Rather than paying out on diagnosis of a defined condition, income protection pays a benefit if the policyholder is unable to work due to illness or injury, subject to the policy's definition of incapacity and the terms of the policy. Because it is not limited to a defined list of conditions, income protection may respond to a wider range of health conditions, including mental health conditions, where the policyholder meets the definition of incapacity set out in the policy.

This is a general description of how income protection works and does not guarantee that any individual claim would be paid. Policy terms and conditions apply and vary between providers. Independent advice from a qualified financial adviser is recommended before making any decisions.

Mental Health Conditions as a Pre-Existing Condition

Where someone has a history of a mental health condition and applies for critical illness cover or income protection, the insurer will consider this during underwriting. Depending on the nature and history of the condition, the insurer may offer standard terms, apply a premium loading, add an exclusion, postpone consideration, or in some cases decline the application. Outcomes vary between insurers and between individual cases.

Questions Worth Asking When Comparing Policies

Anyone researching critical illness cover or income protection may find the following questions useful to raise with a qualified adviser:

  • What conditions are listed in this policy, and what are the specific definitions used?
  • Are any mental health conditions included, and if so, under what circumstances?
  • What does this policy exclude?
  • How does income protection differ from this policy in terms of what it covers?

These questions are provided for general information purposes. An independent financial adviser can help assess which types of policy may be suitable for an individual's circumstances.

Summary

Standard critical illness policies in the UK generally do not include mental health conditions in their list of covered conditions. Income protection insurance works differently and may respond to a wider range of health conditions.
