Life Insurance for High-Risk Occupations: What UK Workers Need to Know

T
Tanweer Hussain
Protection Expert
11 May 2026 4 min read

Your occupation is one of the factors that insurers take into account when calculating your life insurance premium. For most people in standard office or professional roles, occupation has little or no impact on the cost of cover. But for those working in physically demanding, hazardous, or unusual roles, the effect can be significant.

How Occupation Affects Life Insurance Underwriting

Insurers assess occupation risk based on several factors: the likelihood of a work-related death or injury, the environment in which you work, whether you are exposed to hazardous materials, whether you work at height, and whether your role involves overseas travel to high-risk regions.

The outcome can range from no impact on your premium, through to a modest loading, through to specific exclusions, or in some cases a decline. Knowing how your occupation is likely to be viewed helps you approach the market in the right way.

Construction and Trades

Construction workers, electricians, plumbers, roofers, and scaffolders typically face higher premiums than office workers. Working at height or with hazardous materials increases the risk assessment. Most mainstream insurers will offer cover with a loading, though some specialist roles may require specialist underwriters.

Emergency Services

Police officers, firefighters, and paramedics are generally accepted by mainstream insurers at standard or near-standard rates, reflecting the professional training and safety protocols involved in these roles. Specialist armed response officers, hazardous materials teams, or bomb disposal roles may be assessed differently.

Armed Forces

Life insurance for active military personnel is one of the more complex areas. Many standard policies include war exclusions, meaning a claim resulting from active combat may not be paid. The Armed Forces Pension Scheme provides some death benefit, but additional personal cover may still be needed. Specialist insurers cater specifically to military applicants, both serving and veterans.

Aviation

Pilots and cabin crew are assessed based on the type of flying involved: commercial aviation, private flying, or specialist roles such as air ambulance or crop spraying. Commercial airline pilots are often accepted at standard rates by insurers who have specific underwriting experience in this area. Private pilots may face loadings depending on hours flown and aircraft type.

Offshore Oil, Gas, and Renewables

Offshore workers often face higher premiums and some mainstream insurers decline to cover this occupation category. The combination of remote location, physical hazard, and helicopter travel is the primary concern. Specialist insurers and brokers with expertise in this sector can usually find cover, though the cost may be higher than standard.

The Importance of Accurate Disclosure

Whatever your occupation, it is essential to describe it accurately and in full when applying for life insurance. If your occupation contributes to or causes your death, and you had not disclosed it accurately, your insurer may refuse to pay the claim on grounds of non-disclosure. Always declare your actual role, not a generalised description.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to tell my insurer if I change to a higher-risk job?

Once a life insurance policy is in force, you generally do not have a duty to notify the insurer of a change in occupation during the policy term for most standard term policies. However, you should check your policy terms. If you apply for a new policy or increase your cover, your current occupation will be assessed at that point.

Can I get life insurance if I am in the British Army?

Yes, though standard policies may include war exclusions that would apply if you die on active deployment. Specialist insurers exist who offer policies specifically designed for serving military personnel, often without war exclusions. A broker with experience in this area can help identify the most appropriate options.

Does being a nurse or healthcare worker affect my life insurance?

For most clinical healthcare roles, including nurses, doctors, and allied health professionals working in standard settings, occupation is unlikely to result in a premium loading. However, roles with significant infection exposure may be assessed differently depending on the insurer.

What if my job involves significant overseas travel?

Travel to regions assessed as high-risk by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) can affect life insurance underwriting. Some policies exclude deaths occurring in specific territories. If your work involves regular travel to conflict zones or regions with elevated risk, this should be disclosed at application and specialist cover may be required.
