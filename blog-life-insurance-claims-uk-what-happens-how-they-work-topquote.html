Life Insurance Claims in the UK: What You Need to Know

T
Tanweer Hussain
Protection Expert
24 March 2026 4 min read

Do UK Life Insurance Companies Pay Out?

This is one of the most common concerns people have about life insurance, and the data is reassuring. The UK's leading life insurers consistently pay over 97% of all life insurance claims. According to the Association of British Insurers (ABI), life insurance claim payout rates across the industry have been above 97% for many consecutive years, with several major providers paying out at 99% or above.

The most common reason a claim is declined is non-disclosure, the policyholder did not accurately report a health condition, lifestyle factor, or other material information when taking out the policy. Claims are not declined because insurers are looking for reasons not to pay. They are declined when the information on which the policy was based turns out to be inaccurate.

What Happens When a Claim Is Made?

When a policyholder dies, the following steps are typically involved:

  • The beneficiary (or personal representative) notifies the insurer of the death
  • The insurer requests documentation, typically a death certificate and the original policy document
  • The insurer reviews the claim against the policy terms
  • If the policy is written in trust, the trustees distribute the payout directly to the named beneficiaries
  • If the policy is not in trust, the payout forms part of the estate and passes through probate before reaching beneficiaries

For straightforward claims, the process typically takes a few weeks. Complex cases, particularly where there are questions about non-disclosure or the cause of death, can take longer.

Why Writing Your Policy in Trust Matters

A life insurance payout that forms part of your estate passes through probate before your beneficiaries receive it. Probate can take months, sometimes over a year in complex estates. During that time, your family may be waiting for funds they urgently need. It also means the payout is potentially included in your estate for inheritance tax purposes.

Writing your policy in trust removes the payout from your estate. The trustees (people you appoint, such as your partner) can distribute the money directly to the beneficiaries named in the trust deed, quickly and without waiting for probate. This is one of the simplest and most important things you can do when arranging life insurance, and TopQuote facilitates trust arrangements as part of the process.

What Is Non-Disclosure and How to Avoid It

Non-disclosure occurs when you fail to mention a material fact, typically a health condition, family history, smoking status, or risky activity, on your application. Even if it is unintentional, non-disclosure can result in a claim being declined.

The safest approach is to disclose everything relevant, even if you think it might not matter. Our advisers help you present your health history accurately and in the best possible light, but they will never advise withholding relevant information. Full disclosure protects you and your family.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the policyholder dies abroad?

Most UK life insurance policies cover worldwide death unless specifically excluded. You will need a death certificate from the relevant country, which may need to be translated and authenticated. The claims process is broadly the same, though it may take longer to gather the required documentation.

Can a claim be made if the cause of death is suicide?

Most UK life insurance policies include a suicide exclusion for the first one to two years of the policy (the exact period varies by insurer). After this exclusion period expires, claims relating to suicide are generally paid in the same way as any other death claim. If you are concerned about this, our advisers can confirm the exact terms of any policy before you proceed.

How quickly will the payout be received?

For a straightforward claim on a policy written in trust, the payout can be received within a few weeks of submitting the necessary documentation. For policies not in trust, the process depends on how quickly probate is granted. Complex cases or those where there are questions about non-disclosure will take longer.
