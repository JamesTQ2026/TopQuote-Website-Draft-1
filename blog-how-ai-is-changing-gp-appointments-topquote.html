How AI Is Changing GP Appointments

T
Tanweer Hussain
Protection Expert
31 October 2025 3 min read

Technology is transforming how we live, and now it’s starting to change how we access healthcare too. If you’ve booked a GP appointment recently, there’s a good chance artificial intelligence (AI) was quietly involved, even if you didn’t notice.

Across the UK, more GP surgeries are introducing AI tools to help manage the huge demand for appointments. From booking systems to symptom checkers, these tools are designed to make it easier to get help quickly and give doctors more time to focus on complex cases.

From early-morning phone calls to quick online triage

For years, getting a GP appointment meant setting your alarm for 8am and hoping you’d get through on the phone. But many practices now offer online forms or chatbots that ask about your symptoms and help direct you to the right support.

This can mean you’re booked in faster, or even given advice immediately without needing to speak to anyone. NHS England says more than 6,000 GP practices have already adopted some form of digital triage, and for many patients, that’s meant shorter waits and less frustration.

Quicker access, but not everyone’s convinced

AI tools are fast, efficient and available 24/7. They can quickly flag urgent cases and reduce the number of unnecessary appointments, which helps doctors spend more time where it matters most.

But some people find the experience less personal. It can feel harder to explain complicated symptoms to a chatbot than to a person, and there are still concerns about how sensitive information is handled. Others simply prefer speaking to a human being, especially when it’s about their health.

A partnership, not a replacement

Healthcare professionals are clear that AI isn’t about replacing doctors. Instead, it’s about helping them. Many practices use these tools to draft initial notes, flag warning signs earlier, or remind patients about follow-ups. That means GPs can spend less time on admin and more time providing care.

What patients really think

The UK public is still getting used to the idea. A recent YouGov survey found that around half of adults are comfortable with AI helping to book appointments or check basic symptoms. But far fewer are happy to rely on it for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

That shows people value the convenience AI brings, but they still want the reassurance of talking to a doctor when it matters most.

AI isn’t going to replace your GP anytime soon. But it is changing the way we experience healthcare, from speeding up bookings to spotting patterns earlier. As the technology improves and more people see the benefits, AI could become a routine part of how we access care, helping doctors focus on what they do best while making healthcare a little easier for everyone.
