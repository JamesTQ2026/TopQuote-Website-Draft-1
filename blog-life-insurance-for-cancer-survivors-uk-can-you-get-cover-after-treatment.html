Life Insurance for Cancer Survivors UK: Can You Get Cover After Treatment?

T
Tanweer Hussain
Protection Expert
13 May 2026 4 min read

Many cancer survivors in the UK are able to obtain life insurance, though the availability and cost of cover depends heavily on the type of cancer, the stage at diagnosis, the treatment received, and how long ago treatment was completed. A cancer diagnosis does not automatically make life insurance unavailable, but it does make the underwriting process more complex.

How Insurers Approach Cancer History

The central principle in underwriting life insurance for cancer survivors is time since treatment completion. The longer you have been in remission or declared cancer-free, the more favourable the underwriting position is likely to be. Insurers typically want to see a sustained period without recurrence before they will offer standard or near-standard terms.

They will also consider the type of cancer, the stage and grade at diagnosis, the treatment received (surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy), and whether there have been any recurrences.

Low-Grade Skin Cancer

Basal cell carcinoma and other low-grade skin cancers are often accepted at standard rates once treatment is complete, as they carry a low risk of metastasis or recurrence that affects life expectancy.

Breast Cancer

Early-stage breast cancer that has been treated and is in remission is one of the more commonly covered conditions. After a remission period of typically five years, many insurers will offer cover, though a premium loading above standard rates is common. The specific stage, grade, and receptor status of the tumour will affect the underwriting outcome.

Prostate Cancer

Early-stage prostate cancer with a low Gleason score that has been treated and is in remission can often be insured, particularly after a sufficient remission period. Higher-grade tumours or those requiring more aggressive treatment are assessed more cautiously.

Testicular Cancer

Testicular cancer has one of the highest survival rates of any cancer. After successful treatment and a remission period, many insurers will offer standard or near-standard terms, particularly for seminoma cases treated at an early stage.

Haematological Cancers

Leukaemia, lymphoma, and myeloma involve more complex underwriting. Remission periods required before insurers will consider cover are generally longer, and premiums are typically higher. The specific type and treatment history are critical factors.

Pancreatic, Lung, or Advanced-Stage Cancers

These cases are significantly more difficult to insure through mainstream underwriting. Specialist insurers and impaired life underwriters may be able to provide some cover, but it is likely to be at a significant loading and with a lower sum assured.

The Importance of Using a Whole-of-Market Broker

Different insurers take markedly different positions on cancer history. One insurer's decline is not the market's final answer. A specialist whole-of-market broker can approach the market on your behalf without triggering multiple formal applications on your record, and can identify which insurer's underwriting approach is most likely to be favourable for your specific case.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long after cancer treatment can I get life insurance?

This varies by cancer type and insurer, but a remission period of at least two to five years is commonly required before standard underwriting will be considered for many cancer types. Some cancers with very low recurrence risk, such as low-grade skin cancer, may be considered sooner. A broker can advise on which insurers are most likely to consider your specific situation.

Will I pay more for life insurance because I have had cancer?

In many cases, yes. A premium loading above standard rates is common for cancer survivors. However, the extent of the loading depends on the cancer type, how long ago it was treated, and whether there have been any recurrences. In some cases, particularly for low-risk cancers well into remission, standard terms may be available.

Can I get critical illness cover after having cancer?

Critical illness cover after a cancer diagnosis is more difficult to obtain than life insurance, as the likelihood of a future critical illness claim is considered higher. Where cover is available, it may exclude future claims relating to cancer. A specialist broker can identify the options available to you.

What if I am still receiving treatment?

Most mainstream insurers will not offer new life insurance cover to applicants who are currently undergoing active cancer treatment. Some specialist impaired life insurers may consider cover in certain circumstances. Once treatment is complete and you are in remission, the standard underwriting process can begin.
