Life Insurance with Pre-Existing Medical Conditions UK

Can I Get Life Insurance with a Pre-Existing Medical Condition?

Yes, in most cases. Having a pre-existing medical condition does not automatically disqualify you from life insurance. What it means is that your application will go through a more detailed assessment, and the outcome may differ from a standard application.

The three most common outcomes for applicants with a health history are: standard terms (your premium is no different from anyone else's), rated terms (your premium is higher to reflect the additional risk), or a specific exclusion (the condition is excluded from the policy, but you are otherwise covered). Outright refusals are less common than many people expect.

The key is knowing which insurer to approach. Different providers assess the same condition very differently, one insurer may offer standard terms for well-controlled Type 2 diabetes while another adds a significant premium. As an independent broker, TopQuote knows the market and can guide you to the provider most likely to offer favourable terms for your specific profile.

Common Conditions and How They Are Assessed

Type 2 Diabetes

Well-controlled Type 2 diabetes, meaning good HbA1c levels, no complications, and stable medication, is assessed favourably by many insurers, sometimes at standard or near-standard terms. Key factors include time since diagnosis, current HbA1c, any associated conditions (retinopathy, neuropathy, kidney issues), and whether you manage through diet alone or require insulin.

High Blood Pressure

Controlled hypertension, where your blood pressure is well managed through medication or lifestyle, is typically accepted at standard or mildly rated terms. Uncontrolled or severe hypertension is more likely to result in a higher rating. Insurers will want to know your most recent readings and what treatment you are on.

Depression and Anxiety

Mental health conditions are assessed based on severity, how recently you experienced symptoms, whether you have been hospitalised, and whether you are currently on medication. Mild to moderate depression or anxiety that is well managed and not recent often attracts standard or near-standard terms. Severe or recent episodes may result in a premium loading or postponement.

Cancer History

Life insurance after cancer depends heavily on the type of cancer, the stage at diagnosis, time since treatment completed, and whether you are in remission. Some lower-risk cancers (such as certain skin cancers or early-stage breast cancer with several years clear) are accepted at standard or rated terms. Higher-risk or more recent diagnoses may face postponement or exclusions. We have access to specialist underwriters who deal with these cases routinely.

Heart Conditions

Previous heart attack, angina, or stents are all assessable but will require more information, likely a GP report. Factors include the severity of the event, time elapsed, current medication, lifestyle changes made, and any ongoing symptoms. Each case is individual and insurer approaches vary significantly.

What Happens During Underwriting?

When you disclose a pre-existing condition, the insurer's underwriting team reviews the information. They may ask for:

  • Completion of an additional medical questionnaire specific to your condition
  • A report from your GP (arranged and paid for by the insurer)
  • A telephone medical interview with a nurse
  • A full medical examination (for very large sums assured)

This process can take anywhere from a few days to several weeks depending on complexity and how quickly your GP responds. TopQuote manages this process with you, keeping you informed at each stage.

The Importance of Full Disclosure

It is essential to answer all health questions on your application honestly and completely. Non-disclosure, failing to mention a condition, can result in a claim being declined if the insurer discovers the undisclosed condition after your death. The policy will have been based on inaccurate information and may be void. Our advisers will help you present your health history accurately and in the best possible light, but they will never advise you to withhold information.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my premium always be higher if I have a health condition?

Not necessarily. Well-controlled common conditions such as mild hypertension or well-managed Type 2 diabetes are frequently offered at standard terms by at least some insurers. This is another reason why comparing across the market matters, being declined or rated by one insurer does not mean all insurers will treat you the same way.

Can I get critical illness cover with a pre-existing condition?

Yes, though the specific condition will typically be excluded from the policy. For example, if you have a history of heart disease, a critical illness policy may exclude heart-related claims but still cover cancer, stroke, multiple sclerosis, and all other listed conditions. Whether this makes the policy worth having depends on your individual circumstances, our advisers will be honest about the realistic value of cover given any exclusions.
