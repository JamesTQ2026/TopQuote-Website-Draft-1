Life Insurance and Divorce

Divorce is one of the most significant financial life events a person can go through, yet life insurance is often one of the last things people think to review. In the UK, separation and divorce can affect life insurance in several important ways, from what happens to joint policies, to whether existing beneficiary arrangements still reflect your wishes.

This guide is designed to help people who are going through or have been through a divorce understand how their life insurance may be affected, and what separated parents in the UK typically consider when reviewing their cover.

Important

This page is for informational purposes only. Life insurance products vary significantly between providers and individual circumstances. Always speak with a qualified, FCA-authorised financial adviser before making any decisions about insurance cover.

What Happens to Joint Life Insurance After Divorce?

Many couples take out joint life insurance policies, which cover both partners under a single policy. Divorce raises important questions about what happens to these arrangements.

Joint Policy Payout Structure

Most joint life insurance policies in the UK pay out on the first death only, meaning the policy ends after a single claim. Following divorce, this structure can create complications:

  • The surviving ex-spouse would receive the payout, which may no longer reflect either party's wishes
  • Once the policy pays out, no further cover remains, meaning the surviving parent would need to take out a new policy, potentially at a higher premium due to their age
  • If the divorce is acrimonious, having a financial arrangement tied to an ex-spouse can create practical difficulties

Options for Joint Policies After Divorce

There are several options typically available when reviewing a joint life insurance policy following divorce:

  • Cancelling the joint policy and taking out individual policies — this is often recommended, as it gives each party independent cover with their own beneficiary arrangements
  • Policy split — some insurers will allow a joint policy to be split into two individual policies following divorce, though this is not available from all providers
  • Continuing the joint policy — possible in some cases, though the beneficiary and trust arrangements would need careful review

Life Insurance for Divorced Single Parents

For divorced parents who have primary or sole care of children, life insurance is often one of the most important financial considerations. The financial implications of a single parent's death for dependent children can be profound, particularly where there is no significant income from an ex-spouse to fall back on.

Divorced single parents typically consider the following when reviewing or taking out life insurance:

Level of cover

Ensuring the sum assured reflects the full financial needs of dependent children, including housing costs, childcare, education, and day-to-day living

Policy term

Aligning the term with when the youngest child is expected to become financially independent

Beneficiary and guardian arrangements

Considering how the payout would be managed if children are minors, and whether the ex-spouse would have any involvement in managing funds

Trust arrangements

Placing a policy in trust can ensure the payout reaches children directly and efficiently, without forming part of the estate or being subject to delays through probate

Life Insurance After Divorce: New Relationships and Remarriage

Entering a new relationship or remarrying after divorce raises further considerations for life insurance:

New Partner

If you enter a new relationship, you may wish to review whether your existing life insurance adequately protects your new household. If you have taken out a new individual policy, you can update the beneficiary arrangements to reflect your new partner.

Blended Families

Where both partners bring children from previous relationships into a new household, life insurance arrangements can become more complex. Thought needs to be given to how a payout would be distributed between children from different relationships, and whether separate policies or trust arrangements are appropriate.

Remarriage

Remarriage does not automatically update existing life insurance beneficiary arrangements. It is important to formally review and update any trust or nomination of beneficiary documents following remarriage to ensure they reflect your current wishes.

Reviewing Beneficiary Arrangements After Divorce

One of the most important and frequently overlooked steps following divorce is reviewing the beneficiary arrangements on any existing life insurance policies.

If a policy is written in trust, the named beneficiaries on that trust may still be your ex-spouse, even after divorce. In the UK, unlike with wills (where marriage and divorce can affect how assets are distributed), trust arrangements do not automatically change following divorce. This means:

  • Your ex-spouse may still be the named beneficiary of a payout even after you have separated
  • If you remarry, your new partner would not automatically become the beneficiary unless the trust is formally updated
  • If you have children, you may wish to ensure the payout would be directed to them or to a trust for their benefit rather than to your ex-spouse

Reviewing and updating trust arrangements following divorce typically requires professional legal and financial advice. A solicitor or financial adviser can help you understand what changes are possible and how to make them.

Life Insurance and Divorce Court Orders

In some divorce settlements, the court may make orders that relate to life insurance. This can happen in a number of situations:

Maintenance Orders

Where one party is required to pay maintenance to the other or to children, a court may order that life insurance is maintained to secure those payments if the paying party were to die

Mortgage Protection

Where the family home remains in one party's name and is subject to a mortgage, the court may consider whether life insurance is needed to protect the remaining owner

Child Maintenance

Some divorce agreements include provisions for life insurance to protect child maintenance arrangements

If your divorce settlement includes any provisions relating to life insurance, it is important to understand exactly what is required and to seek qualified legal and financial advice to ensure you remain compliant with the terms of any court order.

What Factors Affect Life Insurance Premiums After Divorce?

If you need to take out a new individual life insurance policy following divorce, the same standard factors will apply:

Age

Premiums are generally higher for older applicants. Reviewing and acting on life insurance needs promptly after divorce helps avoid further increases.

Health History

Pre-existing conditions and medical history will be assessed. Any changes to your health since taking out your previous policy will be relevant.

Smoking Status

Smokers pay significantly higher premiums. Some insurers also ask about vaping and e-cigarette use.

Sum Assured

The level of cover needed following divorce may differ from what was in place during the marriage, depending on financial commitments and dependants.

Policy Term

Many divorced parents align the term with when their youngest child is expected to become financially independent.

Occupation

Higher-risk occupations may result in higher premiums or specific exclusions, as with any life insurance application.

Whether to take out joint or separate individual policies is a question worth exploring with an FCA-authorised adviser, as both approaches have different implications for protection and cost.

Typical Next Steps After Divorce

If you are going through or have been through a divorce in the UK and want to review your life insurance arrangements, the process typically involves:

1

Reviewing existing policies

Understanding what cover is currently in place, how it is structured, and whether it still reflects your circumstances.

2

Checking beneficiary arrangements

Reviewing any trust or nomination of beneficiary documents to ensure they still reflect your wishes.

3

Speaking to an FCA-authorised adviser

Who can help you understand your options and compare cover across multiple insurers.

4

Taking out individual cover if needed

Individual policies give you full control over your beneficiary arrangements and are not tied to your ex-spouse.

5

Considering trust arrangements

With professional guidance, placing a new policy in trust can ensure the payout reaches your children or chosen beneficiaries efficiently.

Reviewing life insurance is an important step in the financial reorganisation that follows divorce. Speaking with an FCA-authorised adviser, ideally alongside legal advice, is the most effective way to ensure your cover reflects your new circumstances.
