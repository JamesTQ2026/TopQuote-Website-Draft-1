Life Insurance For Mums in the UK

Life insurance for mums is a term used to describe life insurance policies taken out by mothers who want to ensure their family would be financially protected if they were to die during the policy term. As part of a broader family life insurance strategy, it is something many mothers in the UK consider, regardless of whether they work outside the home or not.

A policy typically pays out a lump sum or regular income to named beneficiaries if the policyholder dies within the agreed term. For mums, this financial safety net can help cover a mortgage, replace lost income, fund ongoing childcare, or simply give a surviving partner breathing space to manage the transition.

Important

This page is for informational purposes only. Life insurance products vary significantly between providers and individual circumstances. Always speak with a qualified, FCA-authorised financial adviser before making any decisions about insurance cover.

Do Mums Need Life Insurance?

Whether or not a mum needs life insurance is a personal financial decision that depends on a range of individual circumstances. However, there are several situations in which many mothers consider it particularly relevant:

Mums with a mortgage: if your name is on a mortgage, life insurance is often considered to ensure the property could be retained by surviving family members

Single mums: where there is no second income to fall back on, the financial impact of a mother's death can be particularly significant

Mums with young children: the younger your children, the longer the period of financial dependency, and the greater the potential need for a safety net

Working mums: where your income contributes materially to household finances

Stay-at-home mums: even without a salary, the services a stay-at-home mum provides have a significant financial value if they were to need replacing

Cover Options For Your Family

TopQuote advisers will help you choose the right combination for your family's situation.

Level Term Life Insurance

The sum assured stays fixed throughout the policy term. If you take out a £200,000 policy over 20 years, that amount would be paid regardless of when during the term a claim was made. This is often considered by mums who want a consistent level of protection, for example, until children become financially independent.

Decreasing Term Life Insurance

Decreasing term insurance has a sum assured that reduces over time, broadly tracking the reducing balance of a repayment mortgage. As the name suggests, the payout decreases throughout the policy term. It is commonly used specifically to cover a repayment mortgage, rather than as a standalone family protection tool, though it may form part of a broader financial plan.

Family Income Benefit

Rather than a lump sum, this policy pays a regular monthly income to surviving dependants until the end of the policy term. Many mums find this structure intuitive, it mirrors the income or financial contribution that would have been coming into the household each month.

What Factors Typically Affect Premiums for Mums?

Life insurance premiums are calculated individually based on a range of personal factors. For mums, these typically include:

Age

Generally, the younger you are when you take out cover, the lower the premium is likely to be.

Health & Medical History

Insurers will typically ask about pre-existing medical conditions and your medical history.

Smoking Status

Smokers typically pay higher premiums than non-smokers due to associated health risks.

Occupation

Some occupations considered higher risk may affect the premium or terms offered.

Policy Term

Longer policy terms generally cost more. Many parents align the term with their youngest child's expected financial independence.

Sum Assured

Higher cover amounts mean higher premiums. The right level depends on your individual circumstances.

Life Insurance and Maternity: What to Know

Pregnancy and new motherhood often prompt the first serious consideration of life insurance. There are some practical points worth being aware of:

Taking Out Cover During Pregnancy

It is generally possible to take out life insurance during pregnancy. Insurers will ask about your health at the time of application, and pregnancy itself is not typically a bar to obtaining cover, though some complications may be relevant to disclose. Each insurer assesses applications individually.

Existing Policies After Having Children

If you already have life insurance before becoming a mum, it is worth reviewing whether the existing level of cover still reflects your circumstances. A policy taken out before children may not account for the additional financial responsibilities that come with parenthood.

After Giving Birth

Many new mums find the post-birth period a natural point at which to review or first take out life insurance. Premiums are generally lower for younger applicants, so acting sooner rather than later can be advantageous from a cost perspective.

Life Insurance for Single Mums

For single mums, life insurance can be a particularly important consideration. Without a second income or second parent to fall back on, the financial impact of a single mum's death on dependent children can be profound.

There are several aspects of life insurance that are worth understanding specifically in the context of single parenthood:

Beneficiary arrangements: single mums need to consider carefully who the policy would pay out to, and how funds would be managed if children are minors at the time of a claim

Trust arrangements: placing a policy in trust can ensure the payout goes directly to chosen beneficiaries and may avoid delays associated with probate

Guardianship: it is worth considering whether a life insurance payout would go to a named guardian, and whether the sum assured reflects the costs that guardian would face

These are areas where speaking with an FCA-authorised adviser and potentially a solicitor is particularly worthwhile.

What Are the Typical Next Steps?

If you are a parent in the UK considering life insurance, the process typically involves:

1

Assessing your needs

Consider your financial commitments, dependants, and what you would want covered. Think about your outstanding mortgage, household income, and how long your family would need financial support if you were no longer around.

2

Speaking to an adviser

An FCA-authorised adviser can research the market on your behalf and explain your options in detail. They can compare policies across multiple insurers and help you understand the terms before you commit to anything.

3

Completing an application

Insurers will ask health and lifestyle questions during the application process. Accuracy is important, as non-disclosure can affect future claims. Your adviser can guide you through what to expect at this stage.

4

Reviewing the policy documentation

Once your policy is issued, take time to read the terms carefully. Understand what is and is not covered, any exclusions that apply, and what the claims process involves. Ask your adviser if anything is unclear.

5

Considering trust arrangements

With appropriate professional guidance, placing your policy in trust can mean the payout goes directly to your chosen beneficiaries without forming part of your estate. A solicitor or financial adviser can advise on whether this is appropriate for your circumstances.

Life insurance is a long-term commitment. Taking the time to understand your options and seek qualified advice is worthwhile before making any decisions.

Common questions about life insurance for mums

Can stay-at-home mums get life insurance? +

Yes. Life insurance is not limited to those in paid employment. Stay-at-home mums can take out cover in the same way as working parents. The key consideration is choosing a level of cover that reflects the financial value of the contribution a stay-at-home mum makes to the household.

Does being a mum affect life insurance? +

Having children does not directly affect life insurance premiums. Premiums are based on individual factors such as age, health, smoking status, and occupation, not on whether or not you are a parent.

Can I get life insurance if i am pregnant +

In most cases, yes. Pregnancy alone is not typically a reason for insurers to decline cover. However, certain pregnancy-related complications or conditions may be relevant to declare. Each insurer will assess your application individually, and some may defer a decision until after the birth.

What happens to life insurance after separation or divorce? +

Relationship breakdown can have implications for existing policies, particularly if you have a joint policy or if your named beneficiaries have changed. It is generally advisable to review your cover following a separation or divorce, and to update beneficiary arrangements where necessary.

Should I get life insurance or cirtical illness cover as a mum? +

These are two different products that cover different risks. Life insurance pays out on death. Critical illness cover pays out on diagnosis of a specified serious condition, such as cancer or a heart attack, during the policy term, regardless of whether you survive. Some policies combine both. Many mums consider whether they need one or both, depending on their individual circumstances.

How much life insurance does a mum need? +

There is no universal answer, it depends on your financial situation, debts, income, number of children, and what you would want covered. Common starting points include a multiple of annual salary, the outstanding mortgage balance, or a calculation based on years of financial dependency remaining. An FCA-authorised adviser can help you work through these figures.
