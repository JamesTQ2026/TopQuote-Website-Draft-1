Do you need life insurance for a mortgage?

This page is general information about life cover. It is not advice, not mortgage advice, and not personal to your circumstances. It does not cover mortgages, buildings insurance or contents insurance, which are separate products. No product, insurer or service is being promoted here, and nothing on this page is an invitation to buy anything. Numbered references link to the source for each statement, and all sources are listed in the appendix.

It is not a legal requirement to have life insurance when you get a mortgage. You might choose to take it out if you want your dependants to have enough money to keep the home when you die.[1]

Why life insurance comes up when you get a mortgage

Three reasons, and only one of them is about you.

  • It is the moment people think about it. Taking on the largest debt of your life is the point at which the question of what happens to it becomes real. That is a reasonable time to raise cover, and nothing improper about it.
  • Your mortgage offer may make some insurance a condition of the loan. Conditions are set out in the offer itself. That is a separate matter from life cover, and worth reading rather than assuming either way.
  • It is sold at the same time. Protection is commonly arranged during a mortgage application because the adviser is already there and already has your details.

The third one is worth understanding properly, and the regulator has said something about it.

In March 2025 the Financial Conduct Authority launched a market study into how the distribution of pure protection products is working for consumers. It noted indications of good outcomes and relatively few complaints, alongside concerns that the commissions used to sell these products may affect the outcomes consumers receive and the products’ value or design. The study is examining whether premiums are being raised by insurers to pay a higher commission to an intermediary, and whether the products provide fair value.[2]

None of that means cover offered during a mortgage application is wrong for you. It means it is reasonable to treat it as a separate decision with its own reasoning, rather than as part of the mortgage paperwork.

Can a lender insist on it?

Life insurance is not a legal requirement, and any condition attached to a particular loan is set out in that mortgage offer. If you are told cover is required, ask for that in writing and check it against the offer document.

If it is a condition, it is worth asking which insurance specifically, because the answer is not always life cover.

Do you have to buy it from whoever arranges the mortgage?

No. Cover can be arranged through a number of routes, including banks and building societies, specialist brokers, comparison websites, directly from insurers, financial advisers, retailers and mortgage providers.[1]

So being offered a policy alongside a mortgage does not mean that is the only place it can be arranged, and taking time over it does not put the mortgage at risk.

If you do take out a policy and change your mind, you have a right to cancel a pure protection contract without penalty and without giving any reason within 30 days.[3]

Do you need life insurance at all?

The bigger question is whether you need cover at all. That has a different answer, and it does not depend on the mortgage.

Cover is worth considering if you have dependants, a partner who relies on your income, or you want your family to be able to cover funeral costs. It may not be needed if you are single, if your partner earns enough for your family to live on, or if you are on a low income and could qualify for State benefits.[1]

Applied to a mortgage, that becomes a specific question: if you died tomorrow, could whoever is left keep paying it? On a sole mortgage with no dependants, nobody is left holding it and the property is dealt with as part of your estate. On a joint mortgage, the other borrower remains responsible for the whole debt.

On a joint mortgage

This is where the answer most often changes. Cover can be written on one life or two. A joint policy is usually more affordable than two separate single policies, but it only pays out on the first death, whereas two single policies would pay out on each death.[1]

If a joint policy pays out and clears the mortgage, it has done its job, but the surviving borrower is then left without cover of their own and would be applying again at an older age.

If you decide to take cover

The amount depends on any debts, your mortgage or rent, the number of dependants you have, and your take home pay or income from other sources.[1] For a mortgage specifically, use the outstanding balance rather than the original loan, and the remaining term rather than the original term. Our worksheet for working out how much cover you need sets out the steps.

On the type: decreasing term cover reduces steadily every year in line with a repayment mortgage while the premium stays the same,[4] which fits a debt that reduces. Level cover stays flat, which suits an interest only mortgage or a need beyond the debt itself.[1]

The payout goes to whoever is entitled to it, not directly to the lender, unless a separate arrangement is in place. Our guide to mortgage life insurance covers how a policy is sized and timed around a loan.

If you already took a policy out with your mortgage

It is worth a check rather than an assumption, particularly if anything has changed since.

  • Does the amount still match what is owed?
  • Does the term still match the mortgage, especially if you have remortgaged?
  • Is the cover level or decreasing, and does that fit the mortgage type?
  • Is it joint or two single policies, and what happens to the survivor?
  • Who receives the money?

Remortgaging is where this most often slips. A new mortgage does not update an existing policy, and a policy set against an old term can end years before the new mortgage does.

If you already hold a policy

Cover arranged with a past mortgage was set to that loan and term. Moving or remortgaging does not reset it.

It still pays out as agreed. Worth checking the amount and the end date line up with the mortgage you now hold.

Things to keep in mind

  • Life insurance is not a legal requirement for a mortgage.[1]
  • Any insurance condition attached to a loan is set out in the mortgage offer.
  • Cover can be arranged through routes other than whoever arranges the mortgage.[1]
  • The payout goes to whoever is entitled to it, not directly to the lender.
  • You have 30 days to change your mind on a pure protection contract.[3]
