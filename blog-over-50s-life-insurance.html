Over 50s Life Insurance Explained

T
Tanweer Hussain
Protection Expert
25 June 2026 9 min read

Over 50s life insurance is a type of whole-of-life cover, usually open to people aged roughly 50 to 80, that pays out a fixed, tax-free lump sum whenever you die. Its main selling point is guaranteed acceptance: no medical, no health questions, and you cannot be turned down. That simplicity is genuinely useful for some people, but it comes with trade-offs worth understanding before you sign up, which is what this guide is for.

One thing to be upfront about, because we are a broker and not a provider: TopQuote arranges term life cover, critical illness cover and income protection, and does not sell over-50s plans. So treat what follows as a plain-English explainer to help you weigh up over 50s life insurance for yourself, not a sales pitch for it.

What is over 50s life insurance?

Over 50s life insurance is a guaranteed-acceptance whole-of-life policy. Unlike term cover, which runs for a set number of years and then ends, an over 50s plan lasts for the rest of your life, so as long as you keep paying the premiums it will pay out eventually. The payout is a set amount you choose at the start, and it is usually modest, the kind of sum people earmark for a funeral or a small gift to leave behind, rather than a figure designed to replace an income.

You will see it advertised under a few names, including over 50 life insurance, over 50s life insurance plans, and over 50s life insurance cover. They are all describing the same basic product.

How does over 50s life insurance work?

The mechanics are deliberately simple, which is a big part of the appeal. In plain terms:

  • Guaranteed acceptance. You are accepted based on your age alone. There is no medical and there are no health questions, which is why people search for over 50s life insurance with no medical. Your health and lifestyle do not affect whether you get cover.
  • Fixed premiums. You pay the same amount every month, and it does not go up. Many plans stop taking premiums once you reach a certain age, often around 90, while the cover continues.
  • A guaranteed payout. The lump sum is fixed from the outset, so you know exactly what it will be. That also means it does not rise with inflation, so its real value slowly erodes over the years.

The waiting period you need to know about

Here is the detail the adverts tend to whisper rather than shout, so we will say it plainly. Almost all over 50s plans have a waiting period, often the first one to two years. If you die from illness or natural causes during that window, the plan usually does not pay the full lump sum. Instead it refunds the premiums you have paid, sometimes with a little interest. Death by accident is normally covered from day one. It is not a catch hidden in the small print so much as a feature of how these plans are priced, but it is absolutely something to know before you assume you are fully covered from the off.

How much does over 50s life insurance pay out?

Less than people often expect, which is the honest answer to how much is over 50s life insurance worth at the end. The lump sums on these plans are generally modest, which is why they are so often framed around funeral costs or leaving a little something behind, rather than clearing a mortgage or replacing years of income. We are not going to quote figures, because what you get depends on the plan, your age and what you choose to pay in, but it is fair to set your expectations towards modest rather than life-changing.

The honest catch: could you pay in more than your family gets back?

This is the single most important question to ask, and MoneyHelper, the government-backed money guidance service, flags it directly: with an over 50s plan you might pay in more over time than your family ever gets back. Because you keep paying for life, someone who takes a plan out early and lives a long time can end up having paid in more than the eventual payout. That does not make these plans a con. For someone who would otherwise leave nothing, a guaranteed payout still has real value. But it does mean the maths is worth doing with clear eyes rather than being swept along by the word guaranteed.

Who is over 50s life insurance for?

It tends to suit a specific situation rather than everyone over fifty. It can make sense if you have been declined for standard cover because of your health, since acceptance here is guaranteed regardless. It can suit someone who simply wants a straightforward way to leave a set sum towards their funeral, without medicals or fuss. And it can appeal to people who value certainty and simplicity over getting the absolute most for their money. If you are in good health, though, it is well worth checking whether other cover would do more for you, which brings us to the alternatives.

What is the best over 50s life insurance?

People search for the best over 50s life insurance, the best life insurance for over 50s and which is the best over 50s life insurance, and the honest answer is that there is no single best or cheapest plan that wins for everyone. The right choice depends on your age, your health, what you want the money to do, and how the waiting period and payout stack up for your circumstances. Anyone presenting one plan as simply the best for all over-fifties is selling rather than advising. The sensible move is to be clear on what you actually want the cover to achieve, then look across the options on that basis.

Is an over 50s plan your only option?

Often not, and this is where it pays to pause before defaulting to a guaranteed plan just because it is the one most heavily advertised to this age group. If you are in reasonable health, standard life insurance, such as a term policy, can sometimes offer more cover for your money, because it is priced on your actual health rather than assuming the worst. And if you have been put off because you assumed a health condition rules you out, that is not always the case: we cover what is genuinely possible in our guide to life insurance with pre-existing conditions. The point is simply that an over 50s plan is one option among several, not the only door open to you.

Already have an over 50s policy? Check it still fits

If you already hold an over 50s or whole-of-life policy, perhaps one taken out years ago with a large provider such as Legal & General, it is worth making sure it still does what you need. Policies have a way of drifting out of sight: the original adviser moves on, life changes, and nobody revisits whether the cover still matches the plan. A quick review can confirm it is still right, or surface that something has changed. That is exactly what our Reconnect service is for, and a good review quite often ends with nothing more than reassurance that you are fine as you are, which is a perfectly good outcome.

The bottom line

Over 50s life insurance is simple, certain and accepts everyone, and for the right person, particularly someone in poorer health who wants to leave a set sum behind, that is genuinely valuable. The trade-offs are equally real: a modest payout, a waiting period at the start, and the possibility of paying in more than your family receives. None of that makes it wrong, but it does make it worth a clear-eyed look rather than an automatic yes. Frequently asked questions

What is over 50s life insurance?

It is a guaranteed-acceptance whole-of-life policy, usually for people aged around 50 to 80, that pays a fixed tax-free lump sum when you die. There are no medical or health questions, and the payout is typically modest, often used towards funeral costs or a small legacy.

Do you need a medical for over 50s life insurance?

No. These plans are designed around guaranteed acceptance, so there is no medical and there are no health questions. Acceptance is based on your age. That is the main reason people choose them, particularly if they have been declined elsewhere.

How much is over 50s life insurance worth when it pays out?

The payout is a fixed sum you set at the start, and it is generally modest rather than large. It tends to be used for funeral costs or to leave a little behind, not to replace an income or clear big debts. The amount does not rise with inflation.

Is over 50s life insurance worth it?

It can be, for the right person, especially someone in poorer health who wants guaranteed cover. But because you pay for life, you might pay in more than your family gets back, so it is worth weighing up against alternatives before committing.

What is the best over 50s life insurance?

There is no single best or cheapest plan for everyone. The right one depends on your age, health, what you want the payout to do, and how the waiting period and cost compare for your situation, so it is worth taking advice rather than picking on the headline alone.

Can you have more than one over 50s life insurance policy?

It is often possible to hold more than one plan, subject to each insurer's limits. Whether that makes sense for you is a different question, and is worth checking as part of a review of your overall cover rather than assumed.

Does over 50s life insurance have a cash-in value?

No. These are protection plans, not savings products, so they have no cash-in value. If you stop paying the premiums, the cover usually ends and you would not get anything back, beyond any specific terms set out in your plan.
